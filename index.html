Multiplication

Visual Calculator

Watch multiplication take shape as groups, arrays, and blocks.

Intended thinking

The Visual Multiplication Calculator directs attention to how individual units form equal groups and how those groups can be reorganized into rectangular arrays. Students can perceive the same quantity through different organizations, connecting repeated groups, multiplication, and the dimensions of an array. The goal is to develop flexible multiplicative reasoning by recognizing relationships that remain consistent as quantities are regrouped, including how signed multiplication changes the interpretation of those quantities.

Factors & arrays

Factoring Calculator

Reshape the blocks. Find complete rectangles and collect factor pairs.

Intended thinking

The Factoring Array Calculator encourages students to attend to the different rectangular arrangements possible within a fixed quantity. As students adjust the dimensions, they can recognize factors as structural relationships between rows, columns, and a constant total, rather than simply numbers that multiply to a product. The goal is to develop flexibility in decomposing quantities, anticipating factor pairs, and recognizing how different multiplicative structures represent the same number.

Algebra

Algebra Bubbles

Lift terms into bubbles and bring like terms together.

Intended thinking

Algebra Bubbles helps students attend to the structure of algebraic terms, distinguishing the quantity represented by a coefficient from the variable structure that determines which terms can combine. By moving and regrouping terms, students can recognize an expression as a collection of related quantities rather than a fixed string of symbols. The goal is to develop flexibility in reorganizing expressions while preserving their value and understanding why certain combinations are mathematically valid.

Expressions

Order of Operations

Choose what combines next, enter its value, and follow your steps.

Intended thinking

Order of Operations encourages students to attend to the internal structure of mathematical expressions, recognizing which quantities belong together and how operations relate within the whole. By selecting and evaluating subexpressions, students can see simplification as a sequence of value-preserving transformations rather than simply following a memorized rule. The goal is to develop flexibility in interpreting, reorganizing, and evaluating expressions while understanding why particular operations can be performed at each stage.

Integers

Integer Battles

See positive and negative quantities meet, cancel, and move along the number line.

Intended thinking

Integer Battles directs students’ attention to relationships between positive and negative quantities, particularly how opposing units form zero pairs and how cancellation preserves the overall value. Animated transformations connect these relationships to signed expressions and positions on a number line, while subtraction introduces the relationship between removing a quantity and adding its opposite. The goal is to develop flexible reasoning about integers by recognizing additive relationships and invariants rather than relying exclusively on memorized sign rules.

Slope & rate of change

Slope Triangles

Move points and slope triangles, build a line, and explore rates and units.

Intended thinking

Connect changes in horizontal and vertical position with slope as a ratio. Moving and resizing a triangle along a line is intended to draw attention to the constant relationship between those changes, while graph scales and units support interpreting slope in context.

Graphs & functions

Vertical Crossing

Draw distance over time, then play your graph to see the point’s motion across the crossing.

Intended thinking

Coordinate a position on a distance–time graph with a moving point. Matching vertical scales is intended to connect graph height with distance across the crossing, while the time sweep invites students to notice when one time corresponds to one position or several positions.
